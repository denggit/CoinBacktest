# Strategic Reset after R20–R22

Date: 2026-08-17

## 1. What economic edge is actually proved?

None. R20 rejects unlevered V10B components, R21 rejects canonical daily
channels, and R22 rejects BTC-led ETH catch-up. No sleeve is eligible for
portfolio construction or holdout opening.

## 2. What is descriptive rather than tradable?

- Daily Long breakouts occasionally capture large convex ETH trends, but the
  result is concentrated in a few 2024 winners and has multi-month gaps.
- BTC impulses often cause ETH to resolve a 1–2R hourly-ATR path within 24h,
  but the same-direction catch-up advantage is too small for 2× costs.
- V10B's historical curve demonstrates that dynamic sizing and convex winners
  can create an attractive account history, not that its typical setup has
  stable expectancy.

## 3. Strongest current candidate

There is no valid candidate. The strongest accepted-looking prior remains the
historical V10B artifact, but its full-window selection, parity mismatch,
top-ten collapse, 62-day flat, 45% positive months, and >20% stressed MDD keep
it disqualified. R22 Long has adequate frequency but negative discovery net
economics. R21 Long has headline PF but inadequate sample and winner resilience.

## 5. Directions permanently stopped

- Full-window-selected V10B reuse, sizing, component selection, add-on, and
  structural-stop rescue.
- D20/X10 and D55/X20 channel grids, filters, pyramiding, and trailing rescue.
- BTC-led same-direction ETH catch-up threshold/target/filter tuning.
- Previously archived completed-trend reversal/acceptance, R17 pullback
  sequence, simple OI release/rebuild transitions, and failed intraday
  compression/auction/impulse families.

## 6. Filter-rescue warning

The research is not currently rescuing R20–R22 with filters; each branch was
stopped at its frozen gate. The older shadow-wick repository branch does show a
material selection-risk warning: many full-window exclusions, feature buckets,
entry policies, exits, and delays preceded its best reported variants.

## 7. Largest gap to the final portfolio

The primary gap is still **edge strength**. R22 shows that enough independent
event frequency can exist, but not with positive stressed-cost expectancy.
R21 shows that occasional edge-like convexity without frequency and winner
resilience is insufficient. Portfolio coverage cannot be solved before at
least one sleeve survives discovery and validation.

## 8. Gap classification

- Edge strength: critical.
- Winner dependence: critical for trend/V10B priors.
- Cost sensitivity: critical for high-frequency R22 and prior impulse studies.
- Frequency/coverage: critical for R20/R21 but not R22.
- Long/Short balance: secondary; no direction is independently approved.
- Exit/position management: not the present bottleneck because raw entry-level
  economics are already weak.

## 9. Next phase

Do not deepen R20–R22. R23 may audit the historical ETH panic-wick Long branch
because it is economically distinct and reports materially stronger cost-after
statistics. It must be treated exactly like V10B: establish provenance, freeze
one existing rule before visible-split outcomes, strip full-window selection
claims, and keep July/holdout sealed. Failure ends the branch without new
wick/session/flow filters.

## 10. Are we moving toward stable profit?

The last three studies improved falsification quality and separated frequency
from edge strength, but produced no money-making sleeve. Continuing would
become research-for-research's-sake if the next round simply invents adjacent
thresholds. A bounded audit of a pre-existing materially stronger prior is
justified; otherwise the program should seek new data/mechanisms rather than
more price-feature variants.
